Lichtschreiber

A 1.7-metre ballpoint pen that leans on the wall and glows

Year
2026
Status
In build
Dimensions
1700 × ⌀133 mm
Medium
Oak veneer over acrylic, spun stainless steel, 8 LED strips on an octagonal post

A floor lamp built as a working ballpoint pen at twenty-five times scale. The body is a clear acrylic tube wrapped in 0.7 mm oak veneer, so it reads as solid wood until the eight LED strips inside turn the whole column into a lantern. The wooden push knob on top is a real switch: press, click, light. The blue cord leaves through the ball tip itself, at the floor, so the pen never stands on its own cable.

02 / 9

Bubblegum

Sixteen glass globes holding each other up. No frame.

Year
2026
Status
Built
Dimensions
⌀570 mm
Medium
16 opal glass globes ⌀150 mm, E27 pendant sockets, textile cable, zip ties

Sixteen globe bulbs, each in its own socket, tied together at the centre so the sockets point inward and the glass forms one loose sphere. There is no frame and no glue — cable tension and gravity do all of it. Off, it is a cluster of dusty-rose glass; on, it is a single warm mass of light at 1800 K.

A flat object that is only there when it is on

Year

Status
Built
Dimensions

A flat square box on the wall whose face is wood veneer, lit from behind. Switched off it is a plain panel; switched on, the grain glows and it becomes the only thing in the room. Light through veneer comes back years later in the Lichtschreiber.

06 / 9

Folded Chair

Four plywood panels and a seat you can fall into

Year
2021
Status
Built
Dimensions
approx. 1040 × 840 × 1210 mm
Medium
Birch plywood, welded steel base

A lounge chair that looks folded: card model first, at the size of a cutting mat, then the same geometry pushed out to full scale in plywood. Four flat panels — seat, back and two triangular sides — meet in a shallow tray tipped back. The welded steel frame underneath only has to keep it off the floor. He added padding; what he actually wanted was leather upholstery, but he did not have the skills and the knowledge for it back when he made the chair. It is a project he may continue some day.

Experiments with one glass ball cut with many faces. An RGB flashlight is pointed into it and the dark room fills with what comes out the other side: scattered points in whatever colour the light is showing, split at the edges, and a haze with no object in it.

Gabriel Meyer — g-magination, Vienna

Everything in this room, he built himself

The practice is object-making about light. Each piece is worked out in CAD and Blender before anything is cut, then fabricated for real: veneer, spun stainless steel, plywood, LED. Around the lamps sit the other things the same hand makes — a folded chair, pulls, a poster, a glass ball in the beam of a torch.
